  • ATM premium = live bid at the strike nearest spot, nearest expiry in the 25-45 DTE window. Falls back to the last trade price when the market is closed and that trade was within the last few days -- shown as c; otherwise the leg shows –.
  • CC reward% = premium / spot. CSP reward% = premium / strike (= collateral per share). Both have an annualized version.
  • Price %ile = today's live price ranked against its trailing ~3 months of daily closes. green ≥75th percentile, red ≤25th, amber between.
  • IV Rank is a proxy: percentile of trailing 30-day realized volatility within its own 1-year range -- not true historical implied vol (Yahoo's free feed has no such series), so it can diverge from real IV Rank, especially around known upcoming events. Colored the same as IV/RV %ile below (33rd/67th).
  • IV/RV = this expiry's ATM option implied vol (call/put average) ÷ trailing 30-day realized vol (30 days chosen to roughly match this scanner's own 25-45 DTE window). >1x means the market is pricing in more movement than has actually happened lately. Colored red <0.9x, amber 0.9-1.3x, green 1.3-2.0x, blue >2.0x. Still directional, not precise: it's a fixed trailing window, not a forecast of realized vol over the option's own remaining life.
  • IV/RV %ile = where today's IV/RV ratio sits against its own trailing 3 months. Also a proxy, and for the same reason as IV Rank -- no free source has 3 months of historical implied vol -- so this holds today's IV constant and ranks against 3 months of realized-vol regimes instead (mathematically, today's IV cancels out of the comparison entirely). Colored red ≤33rd percentile, green ≥67th, amber between.
  • RSI = standard 14-day RSI. Colored red ≥70 (overbought), green ≤30 (oversold), amber between -- note this is the opposite direction from the percentile columns above (here, high is red).
  • F. PE = forward P/E (from Yahoo's analyst consensus forward earnings estimate). Colored red >22, green ≤15, amber between -- also inverted: high P/E (expensive) is red, low (cheap) is green.
  • Bid-ask-spread and open-interest filters apply per leg: a stock stays listed if either leg qualifies, with the other leg blanked. *Yahoo's openInterest field is frequently 0 even on heavily-traded contracts (confirmed on AAPL/SPY/TSLA across strikes and expirations -- a gap in their free feed, not a market reality), so "min open interest" checks max(open interest, today's volume).

US 10-year Treasury yields

loading ...
Nominal 10Y yield (DGS10, amber) -- the plain interest rate on 10-year US Treasury debt: real return + the market's expected inflation + a small term premium for locking up money that long. It's the baseline "risk-free rate" most valuation models discount future cash flows against, so when it rises, every future cash flow is worth less today -- capital has a more attractive risk-free alternative to equities, which pressures stock valuations broadly.
Real 10Y yield (DFII10, blue) = nominal yield − the market's own implied inflation expectation (priced directly into TIPS, not estimated from CPI forecasts). This is the rate that matters most for equity valuations, especially growth stocks: their value sits mostly in cash flows years or decades out, so a higher real yield raises the discount rate applied to those distant profits far more than it does for a value stock earning most of its cash now -- the higher the real yield, the more growth-stock valuations compress (and the more they re-rate higher when it falls).
Both series fetched directly from FRED, not TradingView chart embeds like the QQQ chart above -- their free embed widget refuses these two symbols specifically (confirmed live: "this symbol is only available on TradingView"). Rendered instead with TradingView's separate, open-source Lightweight Charts library, fed the fetched FRED points -- scroll/pinch to zoom, drag to pan, hover for the crosshair's exact date/value on either line. Still fetched, not computed: the Fed publishes both constant-maturity yields directly, this app only draws them (and the implied-inflation gap between them, which is plain subtraction on the two fetched latest values, not a forecast).

US federal funds rate

loading ...
The Fed's own overnight policy rate (FRED DFF, daily effective federal funds rate) -- what banks charge each other for overnight loans, set via the Fed's target range and the primary lever of US monetary policy. This is the rate everyone means by "the Fed raised/cut interest rates"; it anchors the short end of the curve, while the 10-year yields above reflect the market's own expectation for where this rate averages out over the next decade plus a term premium. Impact on stocks: higher fed funds → tighter financial conditions -- pricier variable-rate debt, reduced consumer/business spending, and a richer "risk-free" alternative competing directly with equities -- generally pressures valuations, hitting leveraged and rate-sensitive names (small caps, unprofitable growth) hardest; cuts ease all of that and are typically a tailwind, especially for the same rate-sensitive names. Fetched directly from FRED, rendered with the same Lightweight Charts setup as the chart above (zoom, pan, crosshair) -- not computed by this app.

  • Slow score (tier 1): -4 to +4, +1/-1 per condition below (0 in its neutral zone, or if an input is missing) -- Price/SMA200 >1.03x / <0.97x, SMA50/SMA200 >1.01x / <0.99x, Slope 200 (SMA200 now vs. 20 trading days ago) >+0.5% / <-0.5%, and +DI vs -DI (ADX's own directional components -- whether rising or falling momentum currently dominates). Uses its own SMA50/SMA200 calculated from price history, kept separate from the fetched 50/200 DMA above -- Slope 200 needs the average's value 20 days ago, which no fetched "today only" field can give, so the whole score uses one internally consistent calculation rather than mixing a fetched "today" number with a computed "20 days ago" one.
  • Reversal score (tier 2): two separate 0-5 counts (not netted into one signed score like tier 1) tallying how many of 5 measures tilt bullish vs. bearish -- Price vs SMA20, Price vs SMA50, SMA20 vs SMA50 (all plain greater-than/less-than, no neutral band), +DI vs -DI, and ADX momentum (ADX now vs. 10 trading days ago) >+3 / <-3 ADX points. Each measure counts toward one side or neither, never both. Reuses tier 1's own SMA50 and adds its own SMA20 (same "no fetched historical value" reasoning as Slope 200 above, just 10 days back instead of 20).
  • Market regime checks the reversal counts before the tier-1 bands, so a reversal label overrides what tier 1 alone would say: Bullish Reversal (bullish reversal count ≥4/5 and slow score ≤-2), Bearish Reversal (bearish reversal count ≥4/5 and slow score ≥2), else falls through to tier 1: Strong Bull (score ≥3, ADX ≥25), Bull (score ≥2, ADX <25), Strong Bear (score ≤-3, ADX ≥25), Bear (score ≤-2, ADX <25), else Sideways -- 7 possible labels in total (see the legend table above).
  • The small tags next to the regime badge show the last 3 days' regimes (oldest first, re-derived by re-running the exact same classification against each day's own historical values) -- a quick visual check for whether the label above is a stable read or has been flip-flopping day to day, since a large day-to-day swing in either score can flip the labeled regime even when the underlying trend hasn't really changed.
  • The Recommendations panel and the regime-action matrix below (highlighting today's row) are a static lookup table keyed by the live regime -- not a computed signal like everything else on this tab. New LEAP = new long-dated call entries, PMCC = the short-call leg of an existing poor man's covered call, Wheel CSP = cash-secured put sizing, QQQ DCA = the dollar-cost-average multiplier on the baseline (1×) buy schedule. Purely a starting-point suggestion to translate the regime into portfolio action -- not investment advice, decide and execute manually.
